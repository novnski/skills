# Generic patterns

The modal answers: what appears on a page when nothing was decided. Section 6 of the skill says how to use this list. Any one of these can be right when it follows from the Rules in the brief. Remove each one that does not.

**Structure**

- The stock landing sequence, in order, whatever the product: centered hero with a pill badge, headline, subhead and two buttons; logo strip; three feature cards; stats band; testimonial cards; three pricing tiers; FAQ accordion; gradient call-to-action; four-column footer.
- The split first screen: text on the left, an image on the right, a small interface card floating over a corner of the image.
- Every section built the same way: small uppercase label, heading, gray subhead, grid of cards.
- Sections that alternate between two background tints and are otherwise identical in construction.
- Cards for everything, cards inside cards, a bento grid where a list would do.
- A dashboard that opens with four stat tiles, each with an icon and "+12% vs last month", then a chart, then a table, whatever the user came to do.
- Everything centered.
- A row of three or four big numbers, each over a one-word label, under the first screen.
- Numbered markers (01, 02, 03) on things that are not steps.
- A "Back to top" link at the foot of a page only two or three screens long.

**Color and effects**

- Purple, indigo or violet as the accent, or a blue-to-purple-to-pink gradient.
- The next reflex after purple: deep green with cream or lime, sage, terracotta on warm paper, chosen because purple was ruled out and not because of the subject.
- The reflex after that: yellow with black, justified by an object (a sign, a folder, a tape measure) and not by what the field's real products use.
- The reflex after that: orange, coral or rust, taken from a product that happens to use some or from the page's own generated picture.
- One hue washed over everything: tinted background, tinted text, tinted borders, a dark panel in the same hue, and the accent in that hue again.
- Gradient-filled headline text.
- A near-black page with neon glows, glass panels and blurred color orbs for anything "tech" or "AI".
- A tinted rounded square behind an icon, or behind a number that stands where an icon would.
- Part of the page dressed as a paper object: a tilted sheet, folder, tag, ticket or receipt, with a paperclip, tape, a dashed "perforation", a torn edge, or squared paper behind it.
- A colored stripe down the left edge of cards and callouts.
- A card with both a 1px border and a wide soft shadow.
- Hard offset shadows and black-outlined boxes on a flat saturated ground.
- Stripes, a grid or a dot pattern behind a subject that has no grid in it.
- Light gray text everywhere, below readable contrast.

**Type**

- A default sans in three nearly identical sizes.
- A small styled line above or under a heading: tracked capitals, italics or monospace, often with a short rule beside it.
- A serif headline with one phrase in italics or in the accent color, repeated in every section.
- Dozens of slightly different font sizes, one per element.
- Monospace used to signal "technical".
- Title Case On Every Heading.
- Heavy condensed capitals for anything meant to look bold.

**Components**

- Badges and chips sprinkled as decoration, and a note dressed as a badge: "Sample", "Interactive", "Demo".
- Emoji as icons; sparkles to mean AI.
- A component library's stock theme, unmodified.
- Icons from mixed families and weights.
- An arrow on the end of every link and button.
- Details strung together with middle dots.
- A small trend chart or progress ring beside every number.
- A colored dot beside a label that has no state, or inside a tinted chip beside its own word.
- The same large radius on everything: a rounded card around the first screen and around every block, with pill buttons.

**Content**

- Headlines made of abstractions: "Elevate your workflow", "Built for modern teams", "Unlock the power of…".
- Headlines that promise a feeling: "Peace of mind, built in", "A simpler way to…", "Work smarter".
- The first image anyone would think of for the subject, in stock-photo style.
- A generated drawing in an old print style (an engraving, an etching, a woodcut) of the subject's animal, building or tool, beside the headline.
- Invented social proof: testimonials, logos, "10,000+ teams", "99.9% uptime", star ratings.
- Lorem ipsum, "John Doe", "Acme Inc", "Item 1".
- Twelve features, each a three-word name and one vague sentence.
- The devices listed in `references/copy.md`: a headline of two short fragments, a contrast frame, virtue words, invented specifics, the same cast of sample names.

**Motion**

- Fade-up on scroll for every section, scale-on-hover for every card, floating or pulsing decorations, animated gradient backgrounds.
